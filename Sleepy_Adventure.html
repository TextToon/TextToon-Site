Sleepy Adventure

The room was dimly lit by the soft glow of the bedside lamp. Little Oliver lay in bed, his tiny fingers clutching the blanket as his mother tucked him in.

"Time for bed, sweetheart," she whispered, pressing a gentle kiss to his forehead.

"Will you be here when I wake up?" he asked sleepily.

She hesitated for a moment, her hand lingering on his cheek. "Of course, my love. Now close your eyes and dream of something wonderful."

As Oliver's eyes fluttered shut, the world around him faded, and he drifted into a dream unlike any other.

---

He opened his eyes to find himself standing in a vast meadow, the grass swaying in a breeze that carried the scent of something sweet and unfamiliar. Above him, the sky shimmered with colors he had never seen before—deep purples melting into golden streaks, stars twinkling even though the sun still shone.

A soft giggle echoed in the air. Oliver turned to see a small, glowing butterfly hovering near him. It wasn’t an ordinary butterfly—its wings were made of light, pulsing softly like a tiny heartbeat.

"Where am I?" Oliver whispered.

"The Dreamscape," the butterfly answered, its voice like a melody. "A place only dreamers can enter."

Oliver looked around in awe. Towering trees with golden leaves stretched towards the endless sky, their roots curling like ribbons through rivers of light. In the distance, floating islands drifted lazily, their waterfalls cascading into the air instead of the ground.

He took a step forward, feeling the ground ripple beneath him like water. It didn’t sink—rather, it held him like a gentle embrace.

"Come," the butterfly urged. "Adventure awaits."

Oliver followed, his tiny footsteps making no sound. They walked through the glowing forest, past trees that whispered secrets in the wind. Then, ahead, he saw something that made his heart stop.

A house.

It looked just like his own, sitting alone on a small floating island. The windows flickered with warm light, and the front door was slightly open.

"Mom?" Oliver called out, stepping onto the island. His voice was swallowed by the silence.

Inside, everything was exactly as he remembered. His toys lay scattered on the floor, the scent of his mother’s perfume lingered in the air. But something was wrong—too perfect, too still.

Then, he heard it.

A soft hum, coming from upstairs. His mother’s lullaby.

His feet moved on their own, climbing the stairs. At the top, he saw her.

She sat on the edge of his bed, humming softly, but her back was to him.

"Mom?"

She stopped humming.

Slowly, she turned, and Oliver gasped.

It was her—but her eyes were glowing, swirling like galaxies. She smiled, but something about it felt… wrong.

"Oliver," she whispered. "You’re not supposed to be here yet."

The room trembled.

"What do you mean?" Oliver asked, his voice shaking.

"This is a memory," she said, standing up. "A piece of me, left behind. You have to wake up now, sweetheart."

"But—"

The world shattered.

---

Oliver gasped awake, his heart pounding. He was back in his bed. The lamp still glowed softly, his blanket still tucked around him. But the house was silent.

Too silent.

He got up and ran downstairs. The door was still slightly open. Cold air seeped through the cracks.

"Mom?"

No answer.

The street outside was empty. His mother was gone.

But as Oliver stood in the doorway, something in his pocket buzzed. He reached inside and pulled it out.

A tiny, glowing butterfly.

And in his mother’s voice, it whispered, "Find me."
